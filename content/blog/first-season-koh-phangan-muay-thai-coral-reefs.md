# First season on Koh Phangan: Muay Thai, coral reefs, and island time

I’d been to Koh Phangan before — first in 2005 with friends, then back for Barbara’s 40th birthday, then for the parties: Black Moon, Half Moon, Ban Sabaii after-parties. Never the Full Moon Party. The psytrance parties were the draw.

But 2019 was different. This was the first time I came to train.

## Muay Thai

The gym on Koh Phangan is a meeting point for digital nomads, fighters, yogis, and people who have opted out of conventional life. I started Muay Thai with a skilled trainer, and the training was brutal and beautiful — pad work, bag work, clinching. Your body is your primary tool, and the tool needs maintenance.

What surprised me most was how the discipline of combat training complemented the freedom of the festival lifestyle. They seem like opposites, but they’re the same: total presence, total commitment, total engagement with what’s in front of you. The dancefloor and the ring both demand that you show up completely.

## Morning swims

Morning routine: swim out to the coral reefs. Not laps in a pool — open water, salt water, fish darting beneath you, sea urchins in the rocks, the sun already hot at 7am. The swim is meditation and exercise simultaneously, the ADHD brain focused by the sensory richness of the ocean.

At this point my swimming wasn’t great. I could survive in water but I couldn’t enjoy it. That would change later, after training with Lourens Visser. But even in 2019, swimming to the reefs was magical. The underwater world off Koh Phangan is extraordinary — colourful fish, coral formations, the occasional turtle. It’s the kind of environment that makes you forget you’re exercising.

## The laptop-and-bike lifestyle

Remote work from the island completed the picture. Mornings for training, afternoons for LightSpeed, evenings for the dancefloor or the sunset. The herbal steam bath at a temple for rest and recovery, with massage onsite. During the week I work and train, I live my best life there.

Koh Phangan is a small but not tiny island with a mountain for hiking, amazing beaches if you’re willing to journey to find them. Everything you need fits on a bicycle, and everything I needed was already there.

## The third home

By the time I left in November 2019, I knew I’d be back. Koh Phangan had joined Cape Town and Berlin in the annual cycle. Three homes, three seasons, three sets of training, three sets of festivals. The yearly rhythm was forming — even if I didn’t realise yet that it would become the architecture of my entire life.

Then COVID happened, and everything stopped. But the island was waiting.
