# Berlin summer 2023: 900,000 steps and counting

Every May, I arrive in Berlin and the city immediately sets the tempo. The days are long, the parks are full, the open-air season is in full swing, and the dancefloors don’t stop until Monday morning.

## The numbers

Summer 2023 was my most intense Berlin season yet. Nearly 900,000 steps over eight weeks — close to a million. Most of those steps were dancing. Not jogging, not hiking, not walking to the supermarket. Dancing. For hours. In warehouses, in parks, at open-airs, in basements.

The watch doesn’t lie. When you’re dancing from midnight to noon, you’re covering serious distance without leaving the dancefloor. Add the cycling between venues — Kreuzberg to Friedrichshain, Neukölln to Tempelhof, the canal path at 4am — and the numbers stack up.

## The routine

Berlin has a routine, even if it doesn’t look like one from the outside.

Weekdays: remote work for LightSpeed in the afternoon. Morning cycling. Evening painting prep. The flat, endless Berlin streets free the mind — between Görlitzer Park and Tempelhof, ideas form with every pedal stroke.

Weekends: the dancefloor. Open-airs in Hasenheide Park if the weather holds. Techno nights in the warehouses of Friedrichshain. The after-parties that blur into the next day’s pre-party. UV paint kit in the backpack, always ready.

## Cycling between festivals

The Berlin season isn’t just the city. It’s the orbit. Cycling to festivals across Germany, into Czech Republic, over to Austria. The bike becomes the vehicle that connects the dancefloors. Arriving at a festival by bicycle, dusty and grinning, panniers loaded with paint — it’s become the signature entrance.

## The painting season

Berlin in summer 2023 was also the most productive painting season I’ve had. The full Make-Up Studio Amsterdam mousse palette was in rotation — every colour available, treated like a painter’s oil palette. The darkness of Berlin clubs and the quality of their UV rigs create perfect conditions for the art.

I painted more faces that summer than any previous season. The word-of-mouth effect was real: paint one person, and three more approach when they see the glow under the blacklight. By July, I was painting for hours straight at every event.

## Why Berlin

People ask why I keep going back. The answer is simple: Berlin is the only city in the world where a forty-something South African can cycle to a club in fairy lights with a box of UV paints and it’s completely unremarkable. The city doesn’t just accept this — it expects it.

That’s not tolerance. That’s home.
