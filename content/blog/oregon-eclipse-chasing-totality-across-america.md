# Oregon eclipse: chasing totality across America

In 2001, I watched a total solar eclipse in Zambia from a clearing in the bush, surrounded by a thousand strangers who felt like family. Sixteen years later, in August 2017, I was in Oregon for the same reason: the moon, the sun, and a community gathered at the intersection.

## The Oregon Eclipse Festival

The Oregon Eclipse Festival was an ambitious gathering — a psytrance and electronic music festival timed to coincide with the total solar eclipse crossing the United States. Big Stump, a site in the Ochoco National Forest, hosted thousands of people from across the planet.

The South African contingent was strong. People I’d been dancing with for nearly two decades showed up in Oregon, and the reunion felt like the scene had simply picked up and relocated to the other side of the world. That’s the thing about the psytrance community — the dancefloor is portable. The people are the venue.

## Totality, again

The second time you witness totality, you think you’ll be prepared. You’re not.

The light flattening. The temperature dropping. The 360-degree sunset glow. The diamond ring as the first bead of sunlight pierces back around the moon’s edge. It hits just as hard the second time because the cosmos doesn’t do encores — every eclipse is a one-time event, unrepeatable, a reminder that you are standing on a rock hurtling through space.

The crowd roared. People cried. People held strangers. Sixteen years between eclipses, and my nervous system responded the same way it did at twenty in Zambia. Some things don’t diminish with repetition. They deepen.

## America by festival

Oregon was also a chance to explore the American festival landscape. The production scale was different from South Africa — bigger stages, more infrastructure, a level of organisation that comes from a well-funded scene. But the energy on the dancefloor was the same. The bass was the same. The strangers becoming friends was the same.

I cycled parts of the route to and from the site, which in rural Oregon means empty highways, pine forests, and the kind of vast American landscape that makes a South African feel oddly at home. The scale is different but the feeling of open road is identical.

## The pattern

Solipse 2001. Oregon 2017. Two eclipses, two continents, sixteen years apart. The pattern is clear: when the universe offers you a front-row seat to the cosmic machinery, you go. You don’t check your calendar. You don’t worry about logistics. You say yes and you figure it out.

That’s been the pattern of my entire life. The dancefloor, the bicycle, the paintbrush, the business — all of it started with saying yes to something I didn’t fully understand.
