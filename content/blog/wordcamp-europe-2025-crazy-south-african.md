# WordCamp Europe 2025: the crazy South African on stage

In June 2006, I walked into BarCamp Cape Town and met Dave Duarte, who told me about this open-source CMS called WordPress. Nineteen years later, I was standing on stage at WordCamp Europe 2025 in Basel, Switzerland, as a speaker.

## The WordPress journey

The path from that BarCamp to Basel covers twenty years of WordCamps across four continents. The community that started with twenty-seven people in a Cape Town co-working space grew into a global family. The Theme Review Team. The late-night discussions about block themes and design systems. The hallway track conversations that always teach more than the scheduled talks.

I’ve always been known in the WordPress community as "the crazy South African" — the guy who cycles to conferences, who paints UV faces at afterparties, who runs a profitable agency while living a nomadic lifestyle that makes no sense on paper but works perfectly in practice.

## The talk

The Basel talk was about what it’s like to run a WordPress agency for over two decades while embracing AI, remote-first culture, and a lifestyle that most business consultants would call insane. How LightSpeed has survived recessions, technology shifts, pandemics, and industry upheavals. How freedom and discipline aren’t opposites. How the same brain that builds design systems also paints neon faces at 3am.

The response was overwhelming. People approached me afterward with stories of their own: agency owners who felt trapped by conventional business models, neurodivergent developers who hadn’t been told their brain was an asset, remote workers who wanted permission to build a life rather than just a career.

## What WordPress taught me

The WordPress community taught me radical transparency and knowledge-sharing. Open source isn’t just a licensing model — it’s a philosophy. Build in public. Share what you know. Contribute back. These values mapped directly onto my parents’ values: honesty, hard work, dedication, good business ethics.

The same openness that drives WordPress drives Six Cats (we don’t hide our methods), drives the UV art practice (I share techniques freely), and drives LightSpeed (the team has maximum transparency into how the business operates).

## Basel itself

Basel was beautiful. The Rhine, the old town, the Swiss efficiency that makes everything run on time. The WordCamp was impeccably organised. The afterparty was, naturally, where the real connections happened.

I did bring the UV paints. Of course I did. By midnight, half the WordPress community was glowing.

## Nineteen years of WordCamps

From BarCamp Cape Town 2006 to WordCamp Europe 2025 in Basel. Nearly two decades of community, contribution, and the belief that building together is better than building alone.

The crazy South African is still crazy. Still cycling. Still painting. Still building WordPress sites. And now, apparently, speaking on European stages about it.

The BarCamp that changed everything was twenty-seven people. The WordCamp Europe audience was thousands. The message is the same: open source, open heart, open road.
