## Before the Paintbrush

Before I picked up UV paints in 2019, there were costumes. Twenty years of standing out on purpose, of being the most visible person on every dancefloor.

This throwback video brings together rare footage from the early 2000s Cape Town psytrance scene — a visual history of an identity being built without the owner knowing it.

### The Timeline

**1999-2000: The yellow suit.** Chicken Man era. Found at a charity shop, first worn at Vortex festival. A twenty-year-old in a loud suit dancing like nobody was watching — except everyone was. That was the point.

**2001-2003: The red suit.** Escalation. If one bold suit works, try another. People started recognizing me between festivals. "You're the suit guy." Identity was forming.

**2003-2006: The white and black cow suit.** Character pivot. This wasn't just dressing up anymore — this was becoming a character. People didn't just notice me; they remembered me.

**2006-2010: The brown and beige cow suit.** "The Cow Man" identity fully cemented. At every festival, without fail, someone would shout: "It's the Cow Man!"

**2010-2018: Various themed outfits.** The evolution beyond a single character. Onesies, UV-reactive clothing, painted designs on fabric. Getting closer to the skin, closer to painting.

### The South African Psytrance Scene

The footage captures the golden era of Cape Town outdoor trance: Alien Safari in the Boland mountains, Vortex festivals in the Western Cape hills, the Table Mountain range visible on the horizon.

Two to four festivals per month. Clear stars, proper sound systems, and a community that genuinely cared about each other.

This was the scene that raised me from twenty to thirty. The dancefloor as classroom, gallery, church, and community centre.

### Costumes Were Confidence Training

The psychological insight only landed in retrospect: **costumes were confidence training.**

Every time I put on a loud suit and walked onto a dancefloor, I was practicing something essential — the willingness to be seen. Not just seen but stared at, pointed at, photographed, approached by strangers.

By the time I picked up UV paint in 2019, I had twenty years of practice being the centre of attention. The transition was seamless.

### The Bridge

The bridge from wearing costumes to being the costume was the final step.

The suits and onesies were armour — external identity layered on top of the person. UV paint on skin is the opposite. It transforms the person themselves. No hiding behind fabric. The art is on you, part of you, inseparable from you.

> Twenty years of costumes taught me one thing that made me a better makeup artist: standing out is a skill, and once you've mastered it for yourself, you can teach others to do it.

This footage is a love letter to the community that shaped me, the dancefloors that raised me, and the twenty years of unconscious preparation for the moment I'd finally pick up a paintbrush.

Thank you to everyone who was there. You know who you are.
