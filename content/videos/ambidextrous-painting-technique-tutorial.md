## Painting with Both Hands

One of the most common questions I get: "How do you create such perfect symmetry so quickly?"

The answer: I paint with both hands at once.

### The Discovery

I didn't set out to become ambidextrous. Within the first few weeks of painting UV faces, I realized my hands wanted to work simultaneously — one mirroring the other, creating symmetrical designs from the centre outward.

This wasn't trained. It was how my ADHD brain naturally wanted to move. Both hemispheres engaged. Mirror-image patterns flowing in real time.

### The Technique Breakdown

**Step 1: Start at the centre.** The bridge of the nose, the third eye, the centerline of the face. This is your anchor point.

**Step 2: Work outward simultaneously.** Left hand paints the right side of the face. Right hand paints the left side. The movements mirror each other.

**Step 3: Trust the muscle memory.** Don't overthink. Let your hands find the rhythm. The more you practice, the more natural it becomes.

### Training Your Non-Dominant Hand

If you're not naturally ambidextrous, here's how to start:

1. **Simple lines first.** Practice drawing straight lines, curves, and circles with your non-dominant hand.
2. **Mirror practice.** Draw a shape with your dominant hand, then immediately try to replicate it with your non-dominant hand.
3. **Simultaneous practice.** Start with basic geometric patterns — dots, lines, simple mandalas — and practice moving both hands at once.
4. **Be patient.** It takes weeks of daily practice before it feels natural.

> The ADHD brain that does three things at once has found its perfect outlet: both hemispheres engaged, mirror-image designs flowing outward from the centre.

### Why It Matters

Ambidextrous painting isn't just faster — it creates better symmetry. When both hands move simultaneously, they naturally balance each other. The result is designs that feel organic, alive, and perfectly mirrored.

This is the technique that transformed my UV art practice. Try it. Your brain might surprise you.
