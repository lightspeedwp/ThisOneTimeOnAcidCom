## The Remote Work Rhythm

Koh Phangan isn't just a party island. For three months every year (September to November), it's my remote work base, my training ground, and my creative sanctuary.

This vlog shows a typical day: wake at 6am, train at 7am, code from 10am to 4pm, run at 5pm, paint at sunset. Discipline and freedom in equal measure.

### Morning: Muay Thai Training (6am-9am)

The day starts with movement. Sunrise coffee, bike ride to the gym, two hours of Muay Thai.

Pad work, bag work, clinching, core exercises. The body wakes up. The mind clears. By 9am, I've already accomplished more than most people do all day.

Post-training: coconut water, protein shake, cold shower. Reset and ready for the work day.

### Midday: WordPress Development (10am-4pm)

This is when the real work happens. Six hours of deep focus, building WordPress themes for LightSpeed clients.

**The workspace:** A small beachside bungalow with reliable Wi-Fi, a standing desk, and a view of palm trees. Minimal distractions. Maximum output.

**The work:** Custom block theme development, BEM architecture, accessibility compliance, performance optimization. The same systematic thinking that builds WordPress themes is the thinking that designs sacred geometry patterns.

**The rhythm:** 90-minute deep work blocks with 15-minute breaks. Pomodoro technique adapted for ADHD brains.

The ADHD brain thrives on novelty and stimulation, but it also craves structure. Remote work in Thailand provides both: the novelty of location, the structure of a daily routine.

### Afternoon: Beach Run (5pm-6pm)

After six hours of coding, the body needs movement.

A 5-10km run along Haad Rin beach. Soft sand, warm air, the sound of waves. This isn't training for speed — it's meditation in motion.

The run clears the mental cache. Whatever problems I was stuck on during the work day dissolve with the rhythm of footsteps and breath.

### Evening: Sunset Painting Session (6pm-8pm)

The final ritual: UV painting at one of the beach bars.

As the sun sets, the UV lights come on. I set up a small booth, hang a mirror, and start painting faces. Tourists, digital nomads, locals — everyone wants to glow.

This isn't the high-energy festival painting. It's slower, more conversational. People sit, we talk, I paint. By 8pm, I've transformed five to ten people into walking art.

### The Balance

Remote work only works if you have structure. And structure only works if you have discipline.

**The non-negotiables:**
- Morning training (physical discipline)
- Six hours of coding (financial sustainability)
- Evening run (mental health maintenance)
- Sunset painting (creative expression)

Miss one, and the whole system starts to wobble.

### Why Koh Phangan?

Thailand offers something South Africa and Berlin can't: **affordable stability.**

Rent is cheap. Food is fresh. The weather is perfect. The training facilities are world-class. And the island has just enough nightlife to keep it interesting without becoming a distraction.

Every year, September to November, this is home base. The place where I reset, refocus, and rebuild the foundation for the next festival season.

> Remote work isn't about working from a beach. It's about building a routine that works for your brain, your body, and your creative practice — no matter where you are.

This is the discipline that balances the chaos. This is how I sustain a creative practice long-term.
