## The Journey Is the Destination

In June 2024, I cycled from Berlin to Prague. 350 kilometres over six days. Full bikepacking setup with panniers, tent, sleeping bag, and 40kg of festival gear.

The destination: Transmission Festival in the Czech countryside. But the real experience was the journey itself.

### Day 1: Berlin to Lübbenau (68km)

The ride begins in Kreuzberg. City streets give way to Brandenburg countryside. Through Spreewald forests, along canal paths, past UNESCO biosphere reserves.

Arrival in Lübbenau: a small town famous for pickles and canal boat tours. Camp setup in a meadow near the water. Dinner cooked on a portable stove. Stars overhead.

The body is fresh. The mind is clear. This is going to be good.

### Day 2: Lübbenau to Cottbus (45km)

Shorter day to ease into the rhythm. Flat terrain, long straight paths, endless forests.

Cottbus is an industrial town with surprising charm. Camp near Branitzer Park. Evening walk through formal gardens. The contrast between industrial grit and manicured green space feels very German.

### Day 3: Cottbus to Görlitz (85km)

The longest day. Through Saxon countryside, past abandoned factories, over small rivers.

Görlitz sits on the German-Polish border. The old town is perfectly preserved — baroque architecture, cobblestone streets, a skyline that looks like it hasn't changed in 300 years.

Camp on the Polish side of the river. Dinner in a small restaurant where nobody speaks English and the menu is handwritten. This is the Europe I came for.

### Day 4: Rest Day in Görlitz

No cycling. Just walking, exploring, recovering.

Visit the Landskron Brewery. Climb to the top of the church tower. Sit in a park and watch the river flow.

The rest day is essential. The body needs recovery. The mind needs space to process.

### Day 5: Görlitz to Liberec, Czech Republic (72km)

Border crossing. Germany to Czech Republic. The landscape shifts — more hills, denser forests, villages that feel older and quieter.

Liberec is a university town in the shadow of the Jizera Mountains. Camp in a forest clearing. The temperature drops. The stars are incredible.

Tomorrow: the final push to Prague.

### Day 6: Liberec to Prague (80km)

The final day. Through Bohemian forests, over rolling hills, descending into the Vltava River valley.

Prague appears on the horizon: spires, bridges, red-tiled roofs. The city that's been the destination for six days of riding.

Arrival at the hostel: 3pm. Total distance: 350km. Total elevation: 2,100m. Total time in the saddle: 22 hours spread across six days.

The bike carried everything: camping gear, clothes, food, water, UV paints, festival kit, mirror stand, bike tools. 45 kilograms of gear. Everything an artist needs, on two wheels.

### The Festival

Transmission Festival: three days in the Czech countryside, surrounded by forests and clear night skies.

After six days of cycling, the dancefloor feels earned. Every bass drop lands differently when you've pedaled 350km to get there.

### Why Cycle to Festivals?

The pilgrimage matters as much as the destination.

Arriving at a festival by train or car is convenient. But arriving by bike — after days of effort, fatigue, and slow movement through landscape — transforms the experience.

You're not just attending the festival. You've journeyed to it. You've earned it.

The physical effort creates mental clarity. By the time you arrive, you're already in a meditative state. The dancefloor becomes continuation, not escape.

### The Return

After the festival, I cycled back to Berlin. Another six days. 350km. Same route in reverse.

Total for the round trip: 700km, 12 days, 4,200m of climbing.

> The pilgrimage to a psytrance festival is as important as the event itself. The journey transforms the destination.

This is why I cycle to festivals. Because the art isn't just in the UV booth. It's in the movement, the landscape, the slow accumulation of kilometres, and the arrival at the dancefloor with legs that remember every pedal stroke.
