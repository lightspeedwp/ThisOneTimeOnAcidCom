## The Transformation Is the Art

Here's the insight that separates UV art from every other form of face painting: **what you see during application is NOT what you see under blacklight.**

The transformation is the art.

### Colour Behavior Under UV

This video breaks down exactly how different colours behave under blacklight, with side-by-side comparisons of daylight vs UV.

**Greens:** Look bland and almost military in daylight. Under UV, they become nuclear, radioactive, alive. The brightest, most electric colour in the UV spectrum.

**Pinks:** Seem garish and aggressive in natural light. Under blacklight, they become ethereal, soft, otherworldly. The colour of dreams.

**Oranges:** Look like construction vests in daylight. Under UV, they become solar flares. Pure energy.

**Blues:** Seem dark and flat in natural light. Under UV, they transform into deep-sea bioluminescence. Mysterious and glowing.

**Yellows:** Bright in daylight but muted under UV. Use sparingly for highlights, not base colours.

**Purples:** Rich in daylight. Under UV, they become cosmic, otherworldly, the colour of the void.

### Painting for the Reveal

I learned to paint for the reveal, not the application. Every stroke is placed knowing that the real artwork only appears when the blacklight hits.

It's like composing music you won't hear until the concert hall is full — you have to trust the physics of light and pigment.
