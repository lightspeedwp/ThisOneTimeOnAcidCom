When I cycle to a festival, the journey becomes part of the art. Hundreds of kilometres of road dissolve the noise of everyday life. By the time I arrive, I'm present, clear, and creatively charged.

The touring kit: a gravel bike, Arkel pannier bags, Tubus racks, a bike bag on the top tube next to the handlebars, phone mount. UV paints, brush kit, mirror stand when heading to festivals. Camping gear. Total weight: 40 kilograms. Portability isn't a limitation — it's a design constraint that forces creative efficiency.

I started bike packing in 2012. It evolved from racing into something fundamentally different — endurance, self-sufficiency, meditation, and adventure. The relationship with the bicycle transformed from competition to companionship.

---

The notable routes outside Thailand stack up like stories. 300 kilometres from San Francisco to Point Reyes and back in California — Pacific coast fog, redwoods, and the kind of empty road that makes you feel like the last person alive. Two trips in the Netherlands totalling nearly 800 kilometres, flat as paper but relentless in the wind. Munich to Amsterdam: 1,000 kilometres in ten days, crossing four countries, sleeping in fields and guesthouses, arriving in the Dutch rain smelling like a week of road.

And the Thai routes — over 7,000 kilometres total, detailed in Chapter 9. Those journeys taught me more about self-sufficiency than any book could.

The kit changes depending on where I'm heading. In Berlin, I have the full makeup kit — Make-Up Studio Amsterdam mousse eyeshadows, nearly every colour, treated like a painter's palette. Maximum products, maximum options. In Thailand, I travel light — UV products only. The mousse is expensive and the last time I brought it to Thailand it dried out in the humidity and I had to throw the whole lot away. Learned that lesson once. The UV kit is more portable anyway, and the blacklight conditions at Thai festivals are perfect for it.

I always have a checklist. If I don't have a checklist, I leave stuff behind. Even with a checklist, I leave stuff behind. So every time I do a party, I adapt my checklist. Times change, gear changes, and needs change. The less you carry, the further you go.
