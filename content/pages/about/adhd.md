# Wired different

ADHD isn’t a deficit of attention. It’s a surplus of it, all going in directions the world wasn’t designed to handle. This is Ash’s experience — honest, personal, and completely unapologetic.

> It took me twenty years to understand that this wasn’t broken. It was a feature, not a bug. I just needed to find the right operating system.

## Quick Facts

- **Neurotype:** ADHD
- **Awareness:** Late discovery
- **Superpower:** Hyperfocus
- **Kryptonite:** Boredom
- **Operating system:** Festival dancefloor
- **Creative output:** UV face painting
- **Movement Rx:** Cycling — daily
- **Integration:** Journaling, cycling, creating
- **Work style:** Systems + freedom
- **Amplifier:** Aquarius

## Growing up undiagnosed

Ash grew up in Paarl, a small town in the Western Cape of South Africa, in the 1990s. ADHD wasn’t something people talked about — it was just being "difficult," being "unfocused," being the kid who couldn’t sit still and couldn’t explain why.

School was a battlefield of boredom. He could spend six hours building a perfect model of something nobody asked for, but he couldn’t sit through forty minutes of mathematics. His brain didn’t reject information — it rejected boredom. Feed it something interesting and it would devour it with a focus so intense it scared his teachers.

While the classroom was a struggle, the sports field was where Ash thrived. Racing bicycles since 1994, representing Western Province in cross-country mountain biking by 1995, winning provincial colours by 1997. His brain didn’t need discipline — it needed the right challenge.

## The surplus of attention

The biggest lie about ADHD is in the name. It’s not a deficit of attention — it’s a surplus of it, all going in directions that conventional environments weren’t designed to handle. Too much input, too many connections, too fast for a system built on sitting quietly and following instructions.

For years, this combination felt like a curse. Too weird for the normal world, too scattered for the creative one. The Aquarian need to question everything, to reject the conventional, to see patterns others miss — amplified by an ADHD brain that moves at 200km/h and makes connections across disciplines, cultures, and altered states.

Then the festivals happened. December 1999, Vortex, the Western Cape bush — and for the first time, the world’s operating system matched his brain’s. The sensory richness, the freedom from convention, the community of fellow misfits. The dancefloor didn’t need him to sit still. It needed exactly what ADHD provides: energy, spontaneity, and the ability to process a hundred inputs simultaneously.

## The hyperfocus superpower

When the environment matches the brain, ADHD’s hyperfocus becomes a superpower. Once Ash starts painting a face, the world narrows to just the pigment, the skin, the music, and the design emerging. Hours feel like minutes. Complexity becomes clarity. This is ADHD at its best: total immersion in a task that genuinely matters.

The ambidextrous painting technique is pure ADHD adaptation. Painting with both hands simultaneously in a crowded, noisy, dark festival environment is exactly the kind of multi-input processing that ADHD brains thrive on. It’s not a party trick — it’s a brain that evolved to work in chaos.

The same hyperfocus shows up in business. LightSpeed’s meticulously detailed workflows, the internship curriculum Ash spent six weeks building, the GitHub contributing guidelines, the daily planning templates — all built during hyperfocus sessions. ADHD brains need structure to function, so they build the best structures.

## Finding the right operating system

It took twenty years to understand the wiring wasn’t broken. It was a feature, not a bug. The problem was never the brain — it was the environment. Put an ADHD brain in a school and it fails. Put it on a dancefloor, on a bicycle, in a festival campsite with UV paints and 140 BPM — and it becomes exactly what’s needed.

The artist’s lifestyle isn’t a romantic choice — it’s a neurological necessity. Ash shapes his year around what feeds his brain: cycling for daily regulation, festivals for creative immersion, business systems for productive structure, dance for sensory processing. Every part of the life is designed to work WITH the ADHD wiring, not against it.

This is why freedom matters so much. At LightSpeed, the team gets genuine creative autonomy — not because Ash is a cool boss, but because an ADHD founder literally cannot micromanage. He builds systems that run without constant oversight, hires people who thrive with freedom, and trusts the process. ADHD management style, accidentally revolutionary.

## Movement as medicine

For ADHD brains, movement isn’t optional — it’s medication. Cycling daily in Berlin isn’t fitness; it’s brain chemistry. The 300km loaded-bike pilgrimage to Origin Festival isn’t adventure; it’s the ultimate dopamine regulation strategy. Running, triathlon, Muay Thai, yoga — every form of movement Ash has adopted serves the same neurological purpose.

The festival dancefloor is hours of movement-as-medicine. Dancing to psytrance isn’t recreation — it’s the most effective ADHD intervention Ash has ever found. Rhythm, repetition, physical exhaustion, social connection, sensory richness — everything an ADHD brain craves, delivered through bass frequencies.

Integration is the key word. Not just after festivals, but daily. The cycling to and from painting sessions, the journaling after intense creative work, the conversations with trusted friends — all of it is integration. Processing the ADHD brain’s massive input stream into something coherent and useful.
