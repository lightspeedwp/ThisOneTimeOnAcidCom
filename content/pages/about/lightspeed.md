# The day job

LightSpeedDevelopment — LSD for short. A WordPress agency founded in 2003 that bridges the gap between Ash’s technical professional self and his creative festival self. 23 years of web development, design systems, and open-source community.

> The acronym is LSD. The business started as IT support in 2003 and evolved into one of South Africa’s most respected WordPress agencies. The same obsessive attention to detail that Ash brings to UV face painting, he brings to design systems.

## The origin story (2003)

LightSpeed was founded in 2003 as an IT support company. Ash was 22, self-taught, and driven by the same relentless curiosity that would later define his art. Before LightSpeed, he’d already cut his teeth as a bicycle courier at Peddlars, an IT support intern at DuxTel, a sysadmin at City Varsity, Qeo Wireless, and SynthaSite, and a Scrum Master at Media24. The company name — LightSpeedDevelopment, acronym LSD — was deliberate. If you know, you know.

The pivot came after BarCamp Cape Town in 2006, where Ash met Dave Duarte, who inspired him to explore WordPress. Within months, Warwick Booth joined as lead developer, and LightSpeed transformed from IT support into a WordPress web development agency. That decision changed everything. The entrepreneurial drive came from his parents’ values: honesty, hard work, dedication, good business ethics, and a good, honest set of morals.

## WordPress & the open source community

Ash didn’t just use WordPress — he became part of the community. He organised WordCamp Cape Town in 2011 and 2012, spoke at WordCamp Europe 2025 in Basel, volunteered at WCEU 2024 in Torino, and attended over 20 WordCamp and WooConf events across four continents.

The community provided mentorship, business opportunities, and lifelong friendships. The same networking instinct that connects people on a dancefloor connected Ash to the global WordPress ecosystem. From BarCamp 2006 to Basel 2025, every event opened doors.

At WordCamp Europe 2025, Ash spoke about “Bridging Design and Development: Figma Design Systems for WordPress Success” — the culmination of years building the LSX Design System, an open-source tool for designers and developers.

## Block-based themes & design systems

LightSpeed’s flagship products include the LSX Design System — an open-source WordPress block theme design system built in Figma — and Tour Operator, a WordPress plugin suite for travel websites.

The design system work reflects the same pattern-recognition that drives Ash’s art: seeing connections between seemingly unrelated things, building systems that are both beautiful and functional, and obsessing over the details until everything clicks.

The company publishes themes and plugins on WordPress.org, maintains comprehensive documentation, and contributes to the broader open-source community. It’s the same ethos as the festival scene: share what you have, build together, lift each other up.

## AI workflows & modern publishing

LightSpeed has embraced AI-driven publishing workflows, integrating GitHub Copilot, Microsoft Learn courses, and advanced automation into the team’s daily work. Ash doesn’t just manage the AI transition — he mentors the entire team through it. Everyone at LightSpeed upskills together, learning in lockstep because that’s how the culture works.

The same ADHD hyperfocus that creates UV art on a dancefloor builds meticulously detailed workflows, internship curricula, GitHub contributing guidelines, and daily planning templates. Out of 450 applicants for the internship programme, only 3 made it through — because “a will to learn” is the number one hiring criterion. ADHD brains need structure to function, so they build the best structures.

## What the day job teaches the night artist

The connection between LightSpeed and UV art isn’t a stretch — it’s the same brain applied to different canvases. Design systems teach composition and consistency. Project management teaches reading a room. Remote team leadership teaches trust. All of these transfer directly to the dancefloor.

An ADHD founder literally cannot micromanage. Ash builds systems that run without constant oversight, hires people who thrive with freedom, and trusts the process. It’s ADHD management style, accidentally revolutionary — and it works for both a WordPress agency and a festival art practice.

LightSpeed runs profitably because of the lifestyle, not despite it. A founder who cycles 300 km to a psytrance festival with a 40 kg bike pack is not someone who lacks discipline — he channels it differently.
