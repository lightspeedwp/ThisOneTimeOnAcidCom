## Frequently asked questions

UV-reactive makeup glows brightly under blacklight (UV light) but may look dull in natural light. Neon makeup is vibrant in natural light but doesn't necessarily glow under UV. I use UV-reactive products for festival and club work where blacklight is guaranteed.

Drugstore brushes work perfectly for UV makeup. The key is having the right shapes (detail, shader, blender) rather than expensive brands. I use a mix of both. Synthetic brushes are ideal for cream-based UV products.

For practice and touch-ups, a portable UV torch (395-400nm wavelength) is enough. For full-face work and photography, invest in a 48W blacklight panel. Avoid cheap LED strips—they're too weak for proper UV reactivity.

Cream-based products with strong setting spray. Avoid powder-heavy looks (they cake in humidity). Revlon ColorStay and Make-Up Studio UV paints survive heat and sweat. Ben Nye Final Seal is non-negotiable for all-night sets.
Keep all liquids under 100ml and in clear bags. Use cream products instead of liquids where possible. Pack brushes in a roll (not a hard case) to save space. Bring a solid brush cleaner to avoid liquid restrictions.

Clicks and Dis-Chem stock L'Oréal, Revlon, Essence, and Maybelline. For Make-Up Studio products, check professional makeup suppliers or order online. I stock up on specialty items when traveling to Berlin or ordering internationally.

With proper cleaning, brushes last years. Replace when bristles splay or shed excessively. I've had some brushes for 8+ years. Clean after every use and deep-clean weekly.
