# The philosophy behind the glow

Creative principles, artistic manifesto, and the worldview that shapes every brushstroke

This is the foundation beneath every UV look I create. The beliefs, principles, and perspectives that guide my work — from the dancefloor to the canvas, from Berlin techno clubs to South African desert festivals.

## Core beliefs

These aren't rules — they're the fundamental truths that emerged from thousands of hours under blacklight, countless festival sunrises, and a lifetime of seeking authenticity.

### Art is energy exchange

Every piece of UV face painting is a conversation between artist and canvas, creator and observer, energy giver and energy receiver.

I don't paint faces. I channel energy through pigment. When someone sits in my chair at a festival, we're entering into an exchange — they bring their story, their vibe, their intention for the night ahead. I bring technique, vision, and the ability to translate their internal world into external glow. The final result isn't mine or theirs — it belongs to the moment we co-created.

### Authenticity over perfection

I'd rather create something raw and real than something polished and empty.

The makeup industry pushes perfection. Flawless skin, symmetrical lines, Instagram-ready aesthetics. That's not what I do. My work celebrates asymmetry, imperfection, and the beautiful chaos of human creativity. A smudged line tells a story. A gradient that bleeds beyond its boundary has character. Perfection is sterile — authenticity is alive.

### The dancefloor is a sacred space

Techno is ritual. The club is ceremony. UV face painting is war paint for modern spiritual warriors.

I learned this truth in Berlin. The dancefloor isn't just entertainment — it's transformation. When you paint someone's face before they enter that space, you're preparing them for a journey. The neon isn't decoration. It's intention made visible. It's armor and invitation, protection and vulnerability. Every look I create acknowledges the sacredness of what's about to happen when the bass drops and the blacklights ignite.

### Creativity requires movement

I can't create from a static life. My art needs travel, new landscapes, cultural immersion, and constant change.

Cape Town gives me ocean energy and South African festival culture. Berlin feeds my techno soul and underground aesthetics. Thailand's Koh Phangan brings discipline through Muay Thai and the rhythm of island life. I don't travel to escape — I travel to create. Every city reshapes my perspective. Every festival teaches new techniques. Every culture expands my visual language. The bicycle touring between them isn't downtime — it's active meditation where ideas crystallize.

### ADHD is a feature, not a bug

Neurodivergence isn't something to fix. It's the engine that powers hyperfocus, pattern recognition, and creative leaps.

Society wants me to see ADHD as a problem. I see it as my creative operating system. The ability to hyperfocus on intricate face designs for hours without breaking concentration. The pattern recognition that lets me see geometric possibilities others miss. The restless energy that drives me to master new techniques constantly. The divergent thinking that connects techno music, sacred geometry, and UV chemistry into coherent artistic vision. This isn't a disorder — it's how I'm wired to create.

### Collaboration over competition

The makeup industry thrives on scarcity mindset. I operate from abundance. There's room for everyone.

I share techniques freely. I teach workshops. I answer questions from beginners. Why? Because art grows through community, not gatekeeping. When I help another artist develop their UV skills, I'm not creating a competitor — I'm expanding the collective consciousness of what's possible. The best ideas emerge from collaboration. The strongest art communities lift each other up. Competition creates isolation. Collaboration creates movements.

## Creative manifesto

This is the contract I hold with myself. The non-negotiables that define how I create, why I create, and what I refuse to compromise.

### No commercial compromise

I will never let profit motives dilute artistic vision.

This portfolio isn't a business funnel. There's no 'Book Now' button, no pricing tier, no upsell strategy. I paint what moves me. I collaborate with people whose energy aligns. I say no to commercial gigs that feel soulless, even if they pay well. Money follows authentic work — but the moment you chase money instead of vision, the work dies.

### Experimentation always

I commit to trying new techniques, even when existing ones work perfectly.

Comfort zones kill creativity. Every festival season, I challenge myself with at least one new technique I've never attempted. New color combinations. Untested product formulas. Geometric patterns that scare me. The failures teach more than the successes. The experiments keep the work alive. The day I stop experimenting is the day I become a technician instead of an artist.

### Process over product

The journey matters more than the destination. The painting experience matters more than the photograph.

Instagram wants polished final shots. I care about what happened while creating them. Did the person in my chair feel seen? Did we laugh? Did the music sync with the brushstrokes? Did I enter flow state? Those moments can't be photographed — but they're the real art. The finished look fades when the makeup comes off. The experience of creating it lasts forever.

### Cultural respect and appropriation awareness

I draw inspiration from global cultures, but I do not appropriate sacred symbols or closed practices.

Sacred geometry exists across cultures. Neon aesthetics are modern and universal. But some symbols carry specific cultural weight — tribal markings, religious iconography, ceremonial patterns. I research before I paint. I ask questions. I stay in my lane. Inspiration is beautiful. Appropriation is theft. The line isn't always clear, but intention and education matter.

### Accessibility and inclusion commitment

UV face painting is for everyone. All skin tones, all body types, all identities, all budgets.

The festival scene can feel exclusive — expensive tickets, expensive gear, expensive aesthetics. I actively work against that. I paint all skin tones (UV pigments show differently on different melanin levels — that's not a bug, it's an opportunity). I teach affordable techniques. I share budget-friendly product alternatives. I make space for beginners. I celebrate diversity. Art thrives in inclusive spaces.

### Sustainability awareness

I acknowledge the environmental impact of makeup products and strive to minimize waste.

Face paints come in plastic. Glitter is microplastic pollution. Single-use wipes create landfill waste. I'm not perfect — but I'm conscious. I choose brands with better environmental practices when possible. I use reusable tools. I minimize product waste through technique (less paint, better coverage). I educate clients on proper removal (coconut oil instead of disposable wipes). Small choices compound.

## The worldview that shapes the work

Art doesn't exist in a vacuum. These are the broader perspectives that inform how I move through the world and how that movement shows up in the work.

### Post-capitalist creativity

I'm building a creative life outside traditional employment structures. No boss, no office, no 9-1. Just skills, nomadic freedom, and the courage to design an unconventional life.

This shapes everything. The ability to spend three months in Thailand training Muay Thai while taking remote gigs. The freedom to decline high-paying work that feels wrong. The portfolio as art project, not marketing funnel. The rejection of hustle culture in favor of sustainable creative rhythm. This isn't side hustle energy — it's a fundamental reimagining of what work can be.

### Psychedelic consciousness and expanded awareness

I believe in the value of altered states — whether through plant medicine, breathwork, dance, or meditation — as tools for creative insight and personal growth.

The geometric patterns in my UV work often reference fractal consciousness and psychedelic geometry. The sacred geometry isn't random — it mirrors patterns people report seeing in expanded states. I'm not advocating drug use. I'm acknowledging that some of my deepest creative insights came from experiences beyond ordinary consciousness. The dancefloor at 4am under blacklight can be just as transformative as any ceremony.

### Masculine vulnerability and emotional honesty

I'm a guy working in a female-dominated industry. I'm neurodivergent in a neurotypical world. I'm emotionally open in a culture that tells men to harden. I refuse to perform traditional masculinity.

This creates interesting tension. Some people don't expect a dude to be the one painting intricate UV face designs. Some assume makeup artistry is inherently feminine. I'm comfortable in that dissonance. Vulnerability is strength. Emotional honesty is courage. Rejecting toxic masculinity doesn't make me less of a man — it makes me more of a human. The work reflects that: bold and delicate, geometric and organic, structured and free.

### Gift economy and reciprocity

The festival culture I inhabit operates partly on gift economy principles — value exchange beyond money, reciprocity, contribution without expectation.

I paint faces for free at festivals sometimes. I teach techniques without charging. I share knowledge openly. Not because I'm a martyr, but because gift economy creates abundance. When you give value freely, value returns in unexpected forms — collaborations, opportunities, connections, creative inspiration. The capitalist model says hoard and monetize. Gift economy says share and trust. Both have their place.

### Embodiment and physical practice

Creativity isn't just mental. Muay Thai, bicycle touring, and dance are as essential to my artistic practice as brushes and paints.

I can't create well if I'm disconnected from my body. Muay Thai teaches discipline, presence, and the beauty of precise technique. Cycling teaches endurance, rhythm, and meditative flow. Dancing teaches surrender and listening. These aren't hobbies separate from art — they're the foundation that makes art possible. When my body is strong and present, my hands paint with more confidence. When I'm physically exhausted from a long ride, my creative brain rests and processes. It's all connected.

## The work is the practice

This philosophy isn't static. It evolves as I evolve. What I believed five years ago differs from what I believe today, and five years from now will bring new insights. That's the point. Philosophy isn't a destination — it's the practice of continually asking: Why do I create? What do I value? How do I want to move through the world? The UV face painting is the visible answer. But the real work happens in the questions.
