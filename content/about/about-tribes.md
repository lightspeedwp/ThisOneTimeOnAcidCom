# The tribes that raised me

No artist creates alone — these are the communities, crews, and cultural families that shaped the vision

From Cape Town psytrance circles to Berlin techno crews, from WordPress developer communities to Koh Phangan Muay Thai fighters — these are the tribes that taught me who I am and how to create.

I don't believe in rugged individualism. The myth of the lone genius creating in isolation is bullshit. Every technique I've mastered, every artistic choice I've made, every value I hold — it all emerged from communities. These tribes didn't just influence me. They raised me. They taught me how to see, how to create, and how to belong.

## Musical tribes

The sonic communities that became my spiritual homes.

### Global psytrance family

**WORLDWIDE**

The foundational tribe — where I learned that music, art, and consciousness exploration are inseparable

Psytrance culture isn't just a music genre. It's a global network of festivals, dancefloors, and intentional communities bound by shared values: transformation, exploration, inclusivity, and aesthetic experimentation. This tribe taught me that UV face painting isn't vanity — it's ritual preparation. That festivals aren't parties — they're ceremonies. That the dancefloor is where individual ego dissolves into collective energy. Every psytrance gathering I've attended, from Forest Trance in South Africa to Boom Festival in Portugal, reinforced the same lesson: we're all connected, and art is the language of that connection.

Core values:

- Consciousness exploration
- Aesthetic experimentation
- Sacred dancefloor rituals
- Global community connection

### Berlin techno underground

**BERLIN, GERMANY**

Where hedonism meets discipline and the club becomes a temple

Berlin's techno scene operates by its own rules. No photos inside the sacred spaces (Berghain, Sisyphos, Griessmuehle, About Blank). Multi-day sessions where time dissolves. A commitment to the dancefloor as transformative practice, not entertainment. This tribe taught me that repetition isn't boring — it's meditative. That endurance is part of the art. That you can dance for 14 hours straight and emerge more clear than when you entered. The techno tribe values presence, stamina, and the courage to lose yourself in the bass.

Core values:

- Endurance and stamina
- Sacred space protection (no photos)
- Repetition as meditation
- Transformation through dance

### Cape Town underground scene

**CAPE TOWN, SOUTH AFRICA**

Raw, authentic, African energy in every bass drop

The Cape Town underground scene blends global bass culture with distinctly South African flavour. Origin Festival in the Drakensberg mountains. Vortex in the Karoo desert. Smaller gatherings in Woodstock warehouses and Southern Suburbs gardens. This tribe taught me that you don't need massive budgets to create transformative experiences. The land is the stage. The people are the energy. South African festivals have a rawness you don't find in European productions — less polish, more soul. This is the tribe that taught me to value authenticity over aesthetics.

Core values:

- Authenticity over perfection
- Connection to the land
- Community over production value
- African energy and rhythm

### Koh Phangan party tribe

**KOH PHANGAN, THAILAND**

Where tropical paradise meets bass music and global nomads converge

Koh Phangan is a magnet for global nomads, digital workers, festival souls, and creative wanderers. The island's party culture (Full Moon, Half Moon, Black Moon, jungle parties) creates constant opportunity for UV face painting. But more than the parties, it's the community — expats, travelers, locals, Muay Thai fighters, yoga teachers, artists. Everyone's there for a reason, usually searching for something. This tribe taught me that belonging doesn't require permanence. You can build deep connections in temporary spaces.

Core values:

- Nomadic belonging
- Cultural diversity
- Tropical hedonism with intention
- Temporary communities, lasting bonds

## Creative and professional tribes

The communities that taught me craft, discipline, and how to turn creativity into sustainable practice.

### WordPress open source community

**GLOBAL (ONLINE + WORDCAMPS)**

Where I learned that sharing knowledge freely creates abundance, not scarcity

I've been building WordPress sites since 2003, long before it was the dominant CMS. The WordPress community taught me open source philosophy: collaboration over competition, transparency over gatekeeping, contribution over individual gain. WordCamps (WordPress conferences) became annual pilgrimages where developers, designers, and content creators share knowledge freely. This tribe showed me that you can give away your best techniques and still thrive. That teaching strengthens your own understanding. That community beats isolation every time. I apply these same principles to UV face painting — share freely, lift others up, trust in abundance.

Core values:

- Open source philosophy
- Knowledge sharing
- Collaboration over competition
- Community contribution

### Festival face painters collective

**EUROPEAN FESTIVAL CIRCUIT**

The crew that taught me techniques, products, and the unwritten rules of festival art

There's an informal network of UV and festival face painters who travel the European circuit. We don't compete — we collaborate. Share product recommendations. Swap techniques. Cover each other's stations. Help beginners. This tribe taught me that there's room for everyone. That lifting another artist doesn't diminish you. That the best ideas emerge when painters share freely rather than hoard secrets. Some of my core techniques came from late-night conversations with other painters after the crowds had thinned and we were comparing notes under blacklight.

Core values:

- Technique sharing
- Non-competitive collaboration
- Mentorship and support
- Collective skill elevation

### LightSpeed agency team

**REMOTE (GLOBAL)**

The team that proved remote work, creative freedom, and professional excellence can coexist

LightSpeed is the WordPress agency I've worked with since 2003. It's a fully remote team scattered across time zones, working on high-level WordPress builds for international clients. This tribe taught me that you don't need an office to do excellent work. That autonomy and accountability go hand in hand. That you can have a 'day job' that funds creative freedom without crushing your soul. LightSpeed's flexible structure allows me to spend three months in Thailand or two months cycling between festivals — as long as the work gets done. This tribe modeled what sustainable creative life looks like.

Core values:

- Remote work autonomy
- Accountability without micromanagement
- Work funds freedom
- Professional excellence with flexibility

### ADHD and neurodivergent community

**ONLINE + IN-PERSON MEETUPS**

Where I learned my brain isn't broken — it's just wired different

Finding the ADHD community was life-changing. For years I thought I was lazy, undisciplined, or fundamentally flawed. The neurodivergent tribe taught me that ADHD isn't a deficit — it's a different operating system. Hyperfocus is a superpower. Pattern recognition is a gift. Divergent thinking creates innovation. This community helped me reframe my entire self-concept. The restlessness that drives me to travel constantly? That's ADHD energy channeled productively. The ability to paint intricate UV designs for hours without breaking focus? That's hyperfocus. This tribe gave me language, understanding, and self-acceptance.

Core values:

- Neurodivergence as difference, not disorder
- Hyperfocus as superpower
- Self-acceptance and reframing
- Peer support and shared experience

## Physical and embodiment tribes

The communities that taught me that creativity requires a strong, present body.

### Koh Phangan Muay Thai crew

**KOH PHANGAN, THAILAND**

Where discipline, respect, and precise technique became physical practice

I train Muay Thai every time I'm in Koh Phangan (September-November annually). The gym culture taught me that mastery requires repetition. That technique matters more than brute force. That respect for the art, the trainers, and your sparring partners is non-negotiable. Muay Thai's emphasis on precision, timing, and flow directly translates to UV face painting. A clean jab is a clean line. A well-timed kick is a perfectly placed gradient. The discipline I build in the ring carries into every other area of creative practice. This tribe taught me that the body is the foundation for everything else.

Core values:

- Discipline through repetition
- Technique over force
- Respect for the art and community
- Embodiment as foundation

### Long-distance bicycle touring community

**ROADS BETWEEN FESTIVALS (EUROPE + SOUTH AFRICA)**

Where I learned that the journey is the destination and slowness is power

I've cycled hundreds of kilometres to reach festivals, panniers loaded with UV paints, sleeping gear, and a portable blacklight. The bicycle touring community taught me that slow travel creates deeper understanding. That self-sufficiency is empowering. That discomfort is where growth happens. Touring by bike between festivals isn't just transport — it's active meditation. The rhythm of pedaling clears mental clutter. The physical challenge builds resilience. The landscapes absorbed at 20 km/h imprint differently than landscapes seen through a car window. This tribe values minimalism, endurance, and the freedom that comes from needing very little.

Core values:

- Slow travel and deep presence
- Self-sufficiency and minimalism
- Endurance and resilience
- Journey as practice, not just transit

### Dancefloor regulars and dance communities

**EVERY CLUB AND FESTIVAL DANCEFLOOR**

Where I learned to listen, surrender, and let the body lead

The dancefloor tribe isn't formal — it's the collection of people who show up again and again, who dance for hours, who understand that dancing is ritual. These are the people who taught me that you can't think your way into flow state — you have to move into it. That the body knows things the mind doesn't. That surrender is strength. Dancing for 8+ hours at a time taught me stamina, presence, and how to sustain creative energy without burning out. The dancefloor is where I learned to listen — to the music, to my body, to the collective energy around me. That listening translates directly to painting faces.

Core values:

- Embodied presence
- Surrender and flow state
- Listening to the body
- Collective energy attunement

## Philosophical and spiritual tribes

The communities that expanded consciousness, questioned consensus reality, and explored meaning.

### Psychedelic and consciousness exploration community

**GLOBAL (FESTIVALS, RETREATS, ONLINE FORUMS)**

Where I learned that reality is more malleable than we're taught to believe

The psychedelic community — people who use plant medicines, synthetic psychedelics, breathwork, or meditation to explore consciousness — taught me that ordinary waking awareness is one lens among many. That expanded states can offer genuine insight. That set, setting, and intention matter. That integration is more important than the experience itself. This tribe values direct experience over received wisdom, personal sovereignty over dogma, and the courage to question everything. The geometric patterns in my UV work, the sacred geometry, the fractal aesthetics — they're all informed by what I've seen and learned in expanded states. This tribe taught me that art can be a bridge between ordinary and non-ordinary consciousness.

Core values:

- Direct experience over dogma
- Consciousness as exploratory practice
- Set, setting, intention
- Integration and embodiment

### Aquarian questioners and mystics

**ONLINE COMMUNITIES, ASTROLOGY CIRCLES, FESTIVALS**

Where I learned that cosmic wiring matters and questioning is sacred

As an Aquarius, I found my people in the community of astrology enthusiasts, cosmic thinkers, and mystical questioners who take the stars seriously. This tribe taught me that personality, tendencies, and life patterns aren't random — they're influenced by forces larger than individual choice. That questioning everything is the Aquarian gift. That detachment isn't coldness — it's perspective. That rebellion can be creative rather than destructive. The Aquarian tribe validated my need for freedom, my resistance to authority, and my compulsion to experiment. They taught me that 'weird' is a compliment.

Core values:

- Cosmic perspective
- Questioning as sacred practice
- Freedom and autonomy
- Weirdness as gift

### Festival elders and wisdom keepers

**LONG-RUNNING FESTIVALS (BOOM, OZORA, RAINBOW SERPENT)**

Where I learned that longevity requires balance and that elders hold the memory

Every major festival has elders — people who've been attending for 10, 15, 20+ years. These aren't the loudest voices or the most visible presences, but they hold the culture's memory. They remember when things were smaller, rawer, more experimental. They've seen trends come and go. They know how to pace themselves for multi-day events. This tribe taught me that sustainable creative practice requires rest, boundaries, and the wisdom to know when to push and when to pause. The festival elders taught me that you can be part of the scene for decades if you don't burn out in the first few years. That longevity beats intensity.

Core values:

- Longevity over intensity
- Cultural memory and continuity
- Sustainable pacing
- Wisdom through experience

## Tribes are chosen family

I didn't choose the family I was born into. But I chose every tribe listed here. These communities didn't just teach me skills or techniques — they taught me values, perspectives, and ways of being. They gave me belonging when I felt lost, mentorship when I needed guidance, and challenge when I got too comfortable. The UV face painting you see in my portfolio isn't mine alone. It's the accumulated wisdom of every tribe that raised me. Every brushstroke carries the lessons of the dancefloor, the discipline of Muay Thai, the open source philosophy of WordPress, the questioning spirit of Aquarius, the expanded awareness of psychedelic exploration, and the endurance of long-distance cycling. These tribes didn't just shape my art. They shaped me.
