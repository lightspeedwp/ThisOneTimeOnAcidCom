# Book / "This one time on acid..."

## Overview

Ash has decided to write a book over the next few years.

## Title
**"This one time on acid..."**

## Design
- **Cover:** Neon pink
- **Writing/Text:** Neon yellow

## Concept

People love Ash's crazy stories. He wants to weave them into his biggest life lessons and deepest learnings - the experiences that changed him, made him who he is, and why they impacted him so profoundly.

**Key theme:** It is never one thing. Usually it is the culmination of many experiences over a period that finally crystallise into understanding.

## Core Threads

### Aquarius + ADHD + Lucy
How Ash's Aquarian crazy soul met with his ADHD neurodiverse brain, how these combined with Lucy (expanded awareness/transformative experiences) made him who he is and helped him achieve what he has to date.

### Story Structure
Each chapter weaves a wild story (the kind friends retell at 4am around a campfire) into life's biggest lessons:
- What the chaos teaches you
- How the wiring that felt like a bug turned out to be the feature
- How the festival dancefloor became the classroom where everything made sense
- How ADHD isn't a disorder when your environment matches your brain's operating system

## Timeline
- Writing over the next few years
- Not to be rushed
- Some chapters already drafted in voice notes and notebook pages
- Others are still being lived
- The book arrives when it's ready, not before

## Chapter Previews (Working Titles)

1. **The First Drop** - Every journey starts somewhere impossible. This one started on a rooftop in Cape Town.
2. **Wired Different** - ADHD, Aquarius, and the feeling that the world was running on a different operating system.
3. **Berlin Calling** - A one-way ticket, a bicycle, and the city that would become home.
4. **The Dancefloor Classroom** - When 140 BPM became the heartbeat of understanding.
5. **Neon Revelations** - The night Ash picked up UV paint and discovered his voice wasn't in words.
6. **The Cumulative Effect** - It's never one moment. It's the thousand moments that suddenly click.

## Blurb (Draft)

A raw, honest memoir weaving the wildest stories from the psytrance dancefloor into the deepest lessons they taught. How an Aquarian soul, an ADHD brain, and Lucy in the Sky combined to create an artist, a nomad, and a life lived in full colour.

## What Craft Cannabis Means to Us

Led by a passion for growing and consuming cannabis, we are committed to people, product quality and innovation. Our promise is simple: we will provide homegrown and homemade products, cultivated from carefully selected strains, of the highest quality.

A craft product should be produced with intention, with a connection to the community it’s produced in. We do all the growing and harvesting ourselves, hand-trimming with long curing times to develop a craft flower. Small-batch, hand-tended, high-quality flower grown outdoors using organic and environmentally friendly methods.

From in-house skills and consistent genetic profiles to artisanal methods and locally sourced infusions — quality, trust and choice define Six Cats.

## Cultivation

### Living Soil
We recycle all our food waste using Bokashi bins and a worm farm composter. At the start of Spring each year, we work the organic waste into the soil, adding valuable nutrients, minerals and microorganisms for the new season of growing.

### Rainwater Harvesting
Cape Town was one of the first cities to almost run out of water in 2018. We collect rainwater and store 13,500 litres for household and growing usage. The water is pumped through a triple filter with UV light to ensure a neutral pH balance.

### Companion Planting
Companion planting is the practice of pairing certain varieties of flora to naturally deter pests. We specifically plant basil with every cannabis plant we grow. We also use sulphur and diatomaceous earth for natural pest management.

### Worm Tea
We brew worm compost tea every two weeks throughout the growing season using an aerobic compost tea system. The system efficiently extracts and amplifies beneficial microorganisms from the compost. It must be bubbled for 48 hours for best results.

## Harvest Phases

### Flushing
We start 10 days before harvest with flushing the cannabis plants. Flushing involves running water through the soil medium to remove traces of minerals and other nutrients, forcing the plant to consume stored nutrients in its final week.

### Drying
We hang the plants in darkness in the drying room, kept at 16–18°C with a relative humidity of 55–60%. A fan on low speed ensures decent air circulation to prevent mould. Once the branches snap, they are dry enough to jar.

### Dry Trimming
After about 1–2 weeks on the drying nets, most moisture is gone. We do a very tight trim on our Standard Grade and Topshelf buds, which yields a lot of powdery goodness that we package as our Budget Grade.

### Curing
We place the dry flowers into airtight glass jars. During the first three weeks, we open the jars for ten minutes every day in a dark room. We taste the flowers every week to check their ripeness. After about 2 months we start to package and label — Homegrown Happiness!

## Grading System

**Quads (AAAA)**
*The best of the best!*
This premium cannabis ranks highest in all traits; well cured, with a pungent odour, a sharp flavour, and a smooth, clean-smelling burn & potent smoke. Our hand-selected quads are our best-trimmed buds and cost slightly more than topshelf.

**Topshelf (AAA)**
*Quality fitting for connoisseurs.*
Topshelf will exhibit an aesthetically pleasing cannabis flower structure, demonstrate desirable terpene aromas and flavours, and have a high cannabinoid content. The bud is dense and in good form. No harsh aftertaste is a good sign of this grade.

**Standard (AA)**
*Smaller buds than topshelf, but still top quality.*
Standard Grade has many of the same characteristics of Topshelf, but less bud density. Although it is still of excellent quality, many of the same qualities of the Topshelf bud are still present. The THC potency is also still high.

**Preground (A+)**
*Save time with fine-sieved convenience.*
Stretch your stash with the budtender’s preground cannabis blend. We pass a blend of cannabis through a sieve to remove unwanted plant matter like stalks and seeds, giving you a smooth smoke.

**Budget Blend (A)**
*Perfect for making edibles.*
Budget grade is great for stretching your bud or making edibles. We do a tight trim on our Standard and Topshelf buds, which yields a lot of powdery goodness. This grade also includes some small popcorn buds — you’re bound to find some gold nuggets in every jar.

## Packaging

### Glass & Recycling
Our customers prefer purchasing products in glass packaging. Customers return the jars, which we sanitise and recycle for the next season’s products. Glass is non-porous and impermeable, preserving the taste and aroma of our cannabis for well over a year.

Glass bottles are endlessly recyclable. About 80% of recovered glass becomes new containers or other products. It’s our way of saying thanks to Mother Earth.

### Sustainable Growth
The Humans at Six Cats have an eco-friendly mindset and aim to make environmentally conscious decisions when growing, producing and dispensing our products. We grow our cannabis organically outdoors, using the natural cycles of the sun and seasons.

# LightSpeed / Internship Program - Block Theme Developer

## Background

Ash's previous blog post (https://lightspeedwp.agency/ai-workflow/) started with a comment about how he was in Berlin when he was introduced to some ideas that sparked the evolution of the past six weeks.

He also started looking for new staff during his time in Berlin. He started working on an internship curriculum during that time - it took nearly 6 weeks to put the curriculum together, code the theme produced for the internship, and work out a plan that resulted in two blog posts:

- https://lightspeedwp.agency/wordpress-block-theme-developer/
- https://lightspeedwp.agency/worpress-block-developer/

## Why New Developers Were Needed

LightSpeed needed new developers who were not tainted by the wrong kind of skills. The WordPress industry has a bad reputation because many people use Elementor or other page builders and produce sites that may look good but perform badly.

Building an effective website is not just about building a pretty site and making it move with fancy animations. At LightSpeed the belief is in building sites the right way.

## The Recruitment Approach

Ash Shaw, CEO, founder, and technical strategist, decided to write two separate blog posts to address the skills needed and specifically highlight what was NOT wanted:
- NO Elementor or page builders
- NO people claiming to be WordPress developers who have never used GitHub
- NO people who have never committed a single line of code to a version control system
- In LightSpeed's perspective, if you've never used version control, you're a page builder, not a developer - nothing wrong with that, but not what was needed

## The Application Process

The job was posted on LinkedIn and received **450 applicants**, of which only a handful properly read the requirements.

### Requirements
The job post expressly stated that to apply, candidates needed to:
1. Complete a series of GitHub skills courses: https://skills.github.com/
2. These courses aimed to prime applicants with GitHub version control and Copilot AI knowledge
3. Submit application via the LightSpeed website (NOT LinkedIn direct apply)
4. LinkedIn profile up-to-date (better than a CV in Ash's opinion)
5. GitHub profile showing at least a few GitHub skills courses as public repositories

### Results
- 416 applicants applied directly via LinkedIn with just CVs, no cover letter
- Around 30 people applied with some of what was needed
- AI was used to scan LinkedIn profiles to check if requirements were met
- AI-generated feedback was provided to applicants on what they needed to do
- **Only 2 applicants made it to interview**

# Personal / UV Art — The Origin Story

## The Moment It Started

**July 2019, Berlin.** A psytrance gathering somewhere in the city. Ash picked up UV-reactive face paint for the first time. Something clicked that had been building for twenty years of festival life — from the yellow Chicken Man suit, through the Cow Man era, through years of being the most visible person on every dancefloor — but never having an art form of his own.

That night, the art found him.

## From Costume Wearer to Artist

The evolution was:
1. **Dressing up** — Standing out on the dancefloor through outfits (1999–2019)
2. **Being seen** — Becoming a recognisable figure at parties (the guy in the suit, the guy on the fairy-light bike)
3. **Creating** — Turning OTHER people into the spectacle (July 2019 onwards)

The shift from performer to artist was the final piece. Twenty years of dancefloor immersion, of understanding what makes someone light up under UV, of reading energy and crowd dynamics — all of it was preparation for picking up that first brush.

## The Creative Process

### No Plan, Just Feeling
Ash doesn't sketch beforehand. He doesn't look at Pinterest boards or follow trends. His creative process begins with a feeling — the energy of the music, the light conditions, and the vibe of the person sitting in front of him.

This isn't chaos; it's trust. Trust in thousands of hours of practice, in the muscle memory of his ambidextrous hands, and in the belief that the best art happens when you stop trying to control it.

### The Steps
1. **Read the energy** — Before touching paint, Ash reads the person. Their energy, their outfit, their vibe. The music playing. The light. Everything informs the design.
2. **Choose the palette** — Based on skin tone, outfit colour, and the UV conditions of the venue, he selects 3–5 neon pigments that will create maximum impact under blacklight.
3. **Base layer** — A sponge-applied gradient base, often blending two neon colours, establishes the foundation. This goes on fast while the energy is high.
4. **Detail work** — Fine brushes create the patterns: geometric shapes, organic flowing lines, third-eye detailing, psychedelic fractals. This is where the ADHD hyperfocus kicks in.
5. **The reveal** — The moment under blacklight when the full design comes alive. Colours that seemed subtle suddenly blaze with neon intensity. This is the magic moment.
6. **Capture & release** — A quick photo or video under UV, then back to the dancefloor. The art lives on the skin until dawn, in photos forever, and in the memory of everyone who saw it glow.

### The Tools
- UV-reactive face paints — specifically formulated to pop under blacklight while remaining skin-safe for hours of dancing
- High-pigment neon palettes
- Fine-detail brushes
- Sponge applicators for base gradients
- Everything fits in a single bag that straps to the bicycle
- Portability isn't a limitation — it's a design constraint that forces creative efficiency

### Ambidextrous Application
Festival energy moves fast, and so does Ash. He's trained himself to be ambidextrous, allowing him to paint with flow and precision even in the most crowded, high-energy environments. This isn't a party trick — it's a practical adaptation to painting in chaos. Symmetrical designs, third-eye patterns, complex geometric work — all done with both hands, simultaneously, on a moving dancefloor.

## Painting for the Reveal, Not the Application

UV art has a unique property: what you see during application is NOT what you see under blacklight. Colours that look muted in daylight become explosively vivid under UV. Understanding this transformation is central to Ash's process — he paints for the reveal, not the application.

This requires a mental map of how each pigment transforms. Certain greens that look bland in daylight become nuclear under UV. Pinks that seem aggressive in natural light become ethereal. The experienced UV artist is painting a version of reality that doesn't exist yet — and trusting the blacklight to complete the work.

## What UV Art Means

"I don't just paint faces; I unlock avatars. I focus on eyes and face because that's the centre of connection on the dancefloor."

After the transformation, the person isn't just a spectator; they're part of the visual spectacle of the event. Ash captures the moment, and they stay connected through the global tribe.

## The Connection to Expanded Awareness

The connection between expanded states of awareness and UV art is direct. When Ash paints someone's face, he's not decorating — he's translating. Translating the visual language of heightened perception into pigment that anyone can see, especially under blacklight.

Certain transformative experiences permanently upgraded the resolution at which he perceives the visual world. The way neon paint bleeds under UV light, the way colours breathe and pulse on a dancefloor — these are things Ash noticed because his awareness had been opened to what was always there.

Every neon stroke carries a piece of those experiences. Every glowing face on the dancefloor is a small portal into that wider, more vivid way of seeing. That's the real art — not the paint, but the perception it represents.

### Project Workflows Series Post

At LightSpeed, we believe the difference between a smooth website launch and a stressful one lies in the process. That's why we've created a new Project Workflows blog category, sharing the exact steps we use to deliver WordPress and WooCommerce projects on time, with less risk and better collaboration.

Our framework is designed around two core principles: content-first planning (ensuring real words and assets guide design decisions) and feedback-driven checkpoints (so clients are never surprised at the end). This reduces rework, accelerates timelines, and builds trust.

Here's how it works in practice:
- Discovery — Clarifying goals, audiences, and success metrics.
- Content Collection — Prioritising real copy, images, and media up front.
- Prototyping — Interactive design walkthroughs that align stakeholders.
- Staging Feedback — Structured reviews of the working site before launch.
- Launch — A controlled go-live with checks, balances, and no surprises.
- Client Handover — Equipping teams with knowledge, training, and ongoing support.
