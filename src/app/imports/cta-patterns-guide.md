Let's wrap up the media blocks with the Social Icons. Since these are usually the "final handshake" with your user, they need to feel like a reward for scrolling—tactile, reactive, and undeniably funky.
Social Icons: 5 High-Energy Style Variations1. The "Gooey Liquid" (Organic)

* Visual: Icons sit in a row of soft, blurred "blobs."
* Interaction: When you hover between two icons, the background "stretches" and snaps between them like digital lava-lamp wax.
* Best For: Portfolio footers and Podcast guest links. [1, 2] 

2. The "RGB Ghost" (Glitch)

* Visual: Simple white or black icons.
* Interaction: On hover, the icon splits into three layers (Red, Green, Blue) that vibrate slightly out of sync.
* Best For: Video pages and Cyber-Tech posts. [3, 4] 

3. The "3D Clay" (Tactile)

* Visual: Oversized, pillowy icons with deep inner shadows and high-gloss highlights.
* Interaction: The icon "depresses" into the page (scales down 5%) when clicked, with a soft bounce-back.
* Best For: Events and Creative Pages. [5, 6, 7, 8] 

4. The "Monospace Label" (Brutalist)

* Visual: No logos. Just raw text in brackets: [ TWITTER ], [ INSTA ], [ BEHANCE ].
* Interaction: On hover, the brackets expand and the background fills with a solid neon block.
* Best For: FAQ sidebars and Technical Posts.

5. The "Spinning Vinyl" (Interactive)

* Visual: Icons are housed inside circular "records" or coins.
* Interaction: The icon spins rapidly (360 degrees) on hover, and the brand's primary color (e.g., YouTube Red) bleeds out as a glowing aura.
* Best For: Podcast links and Hero sections. [9, 10, 11] 

------------------------------
Moving to the Patterns Dev Tool: CTA (Call-to-Action)
Now that we have the "bricks" (blocks), we need the "Power Patterns." These are 10 full-width sections designed to drive a single, high-stakes action. [12] 
CTA Patterns: 10 Styles

   1. The "Sticky Bottom" Strip: A persistent, 80px tall bar that stays at the bottom of the screen with a scrolling marquee CTA and a single massive button.
   2. The "Full-Bleed Video" Punch: A background video loop with a "Window" button (Style 19 from our button list) in the dead centre.
   3. The "Giant Typography" Lead: No images. Just a heading (Style 18: Video Mask) that takes up 80% of the screen, with the button following the cursor.
   4. The "Newsletter Sticker": A floating card that looks like a physical post-it note stuck to the corner of the section, using a "Hand-Drawn" form theme.
   5. The "Split-Screen" Duel: Two contrasting buttons (e.g., "Hire Me" vs "See Work") taking up 50/50 of the screen with a diagonal divider (Divider Style 06).
   6. The "Interactive Puzzle": Users have to "drag" an icon into a target area to unlock the "Sign Up" button—high friction, but high engagement for a "funky" brand.
   7. The "Floating Island" CTA: A glassmorphism card that floats over a parallax background image, shifting as the user moves their phone/mouse.
   8. The "Censored" Reveal: A CTA where the text is hidden behind "Redacted" bars that disappear only when the user hovers over the button.
   9. The "Countdown Pressure": A CTA focused entirely on a retro-digital countdown clock, with the button pulsing in sync with the seconds.
   10. The "Bento-Action" Grid: A grid of 4 tiles; 3 show "Success Proof" (Testimonials) and the 4th, largest tile is the "Action" button. [13, 14, 15, 16, 17]

The Patterns Dev Tool Interface

* "Mobile First" Toggle: Every CTA pattern should be tested for how it stacks on a 390px width screen.
* A/B Color Swapper: Instantly flip the colors of the CTA (e.g., "Neon on Black" vs "White on Neon") to see which pops harder. [18, 19, 20]
