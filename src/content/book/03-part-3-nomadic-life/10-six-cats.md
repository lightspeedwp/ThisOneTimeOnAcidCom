I've always had green fingers. Long before Six Cats had a name or a website or a grading system, there was a garden in Cape Town and a quiet, patient relationship with growing that had been building for nearly two decades. Cannabis cultivation is an art form that rewards exactly the qualities I have in abundance: meticulousness, patience, obsessive attention to detail, and a genuine respect for the natural process.

In May 2019, that passion became a club. Six Cats Cannabis Club — named, naturally, for the cats. The first cats were adopted in 2003, a brother and sister called Bart and Lisa. They quickly became the mascots for the yearly harvest. Over the years, nine homeless cats found their way to us, some have left, and the ones who remain are the soul of the operation.

The cultivation philosophy is entirely organic: living soil built from Bokashi composting and worm farming, rainwater harvested and triple-filtered (Cape Town almost ran out of water in 2018 — we take water seriously), companion planting with basil, worm compost tea brewed every two weeks throughout the growing season. Nothing rushed. Nothing synthetic. Small-batch, hand-tended, high-quality flower grown outdoors using the natural cycles of the sun and seasons.

— THE CATS: RULERS OF THE HUMANS —

Timmy — Timmmaaaahh. The sole remaining member of the original six. Timmy has this innate ability to sense when someone needs a cuddle — he'll appear on your lap during a stressful work call or headbutt your shin at exactly the moment you're about to lose it. He's gentle, loving, and provides comfort to everyone who walks through the door. Every harvest season, Timmy claims a spot in the drying room and supervises the entire operation. I'm fairly certain he thinks he's in charge.

Wendy — Wendells, the Agile Matriarch. Rescued during a rainstorm at five weeks old. Five weeks. She was tiny, soaked, and furious about the whole situation. Within a year she owned every room in the house. When Lucy passed, Wendy stepped up as matriarch without being asked. She's a master at soccer with rolled-up paper balls — but engage on her terms, because those claws are real. She's old, fast, and takes no nonsense from anyone, including me.

Jimmy — Super Slim Jim, the FIV Fighter. Jimmy is our FIV-positive cat. The vet said to keep him comfortable. That was years ago. In late 2023 he got seriously ill — we nearly lost him. He made a remarkable recovery and is still here, still eating, still fighting, still claiming the best sunny spot in the garden every morning. Jimmy is proof that a diagnosis is not a destiny. He's the most determined cat I've ever known.

---

Bean — The Survivor. Rescued in January 2022 from under a Wendy house at a local shopping centre, shortly after we lost Moe. She was skittish, feral, and wanted nothing to do with humans. The first two months were slow — leaving food near her hiding spot, sitting quietly in the same room without approaching, waiting for her to decide when she was ready. When she finally climbed onto my lap, it was one of the most rewarding moments of the entire Six Cats journey. She's unrecognisable now. Greets visitors at the door like she owns the place. Because she does.

Jeff — The Wanderer. Found wandering alone in May 2022. He nestled into my arms with immediate trust, which was unusual — most rescues take weeks to warm up. Jeff is the adventurer. He disappears for hours, sometimes days, returning with the air of someone who's been on a tremendous journey and has no intention of telling you about it. His return is always a heartwarming moment. Jeff taught me that not every personality needs to be domesticated. Some spirits need to roam. You just make sure the door is always open.

Frank — Blue Eyes. Named after Sinatra, obviously. Pure white with piercing blue eyes that stop visitors in their tracks. He's not quite a permanent member of the pack — more of an enigmatic stray who's been gracing the household for over a year. His peaceful demeanour and ability to coexist harmoniously make him a valued visitor. Frank is the calm at the centre of the operation — beautiful, untroubled, and content. The universe decided to send us one cat who didn't need fixing.

— IN MEMORIAM —

Lisa (Granny Cat) — Passed July 2020. Lisa was one of the original two cats, adopted in 2003 alongside her brother Bart. She lived to a grand old age and earned the nickname Granny Cat in her final years. She was slow, dignified, and had a particular fondness for sitting in the exact spot where you needed to work. Lisa set the tone for everything that followed — the idea that rescued animals deserve not just shelter but a life of comfort and dignity. When she passed, the garden felt emptier for months.

Moe (Fat-Boy-Fat) — Passed January 2022. Moe earned his nickname honestly. He was enormous, unapologetic, and ate with the enthusiasm of someone who had once known hunger and decided it would never happen again. Moe was the comic relief of Six Cats — the cat who got stuck in places, who fell off shelves, who ate food that wasn't his and looked at you as if to say, "And what are you going to do about it?" Behind the comedy was a cat who had been abandoned and found safety. Moe reminded us that joy is a survival mechanism.

Lucy (Dennis the Menace) — Passed October 2023. Lucy was a cross-Burmese cat with a personality that could fill a stadium. Nicknamed Dennis the Menace for her relentless energy, her mischief, and her ability to be in exactly the wrong place at exactly the wrong time. Lucy was the heartbeat of Six Cats. She was curious, demanding, affectionate on her terms, and utterly impossible to ignore. Her passing hit harder than any of the others. The book is dedicated to her. The green garden carries on, but it carries on with Lucy's spirit woven into every harvest.

— THE EIGHT VALUES —

I didn't write these values down until someone asked. But they were always there — in the way I checked the soil every morning, in the way I refused to cut corners during curing, in the way the notebook got thicker every season. Eight values. They map to how I run LightSpeed too, which probably says more about me than any personality test:

Authenticity — We grow what we say we grow. No relabelling, no exaggeration. If the jar says Amnesia Haze, that's what's inside. Same principle I apply to code — what the client sees is what they get.

Consciousness — Every decision considers impact: soil, water table, neighbourhood, the people who consume the product. Not a marketing term. A daily practice.

Consistency — Same soil, same methods, same care, every season. The goal is not to reinvent but to refine. Consistency is what separates a hobbyist from a craftsperson. Same reason I obsess over design systems at work — the details compound.

Experience — Nearly twenty years of growing. Experience is the compound interest of patience. Every mistake taught something. Every season got a little better.

---

Meticulousness — Every plant is observed daily. Leaf colour, soil moisture, pest activity, growth rate. The notebook is sacred. This is the ADHD brain at its best — hyperfocused on something it genuinely cares about, catching details that a casual observer would miss entirely.

Sustainability — Rainwater harvesting, Bokashi composting, worm farming, glass packaging returned and reused. Nothing leaves the operation that can be cycled back into it. Cape Town almost ran out of water in 2018 — we take sustainability personally, not philosophically.

Passion — This is not a side hustle. This is a practice, a meditation, a way of engaging with the natural world. The same hands that paint neon faces at 3am are the same hands that check soil pH at 7am. Same passion, different medium.

Quality — The grading system exists because quality is measurable. Every batch is taste-tested weekly during the two-month curing process. We don't release anything we wouldn't smoke ourselves. Full stop.

— HOW WE GROW —

The soil obsession came first. I'm not someone who buys bags of potting mix and hopes for the best. We build our soil from scratch — Bokashi bins handle kitchen waste, breaking it down into pre-compost that feeds the worm farm. The worms produce castings and liquid fertiliser. It's a closed-loop system: the garden feeds itself. I lost an early crop because I didn't understand soil biology. That failure made me obsessive about getting it right. Now the soil is alive — teeming with microorganisms, fungi, and beneficial bacteria that you simply cannot replicate with synthetic fertiliser.

The water system was born from necessity. When Cape Town nearly ran out of water in 2018, every resident had to rethink their relationship with H2O. We installed 13,500 litres of rainwater storage with triple filtration — sediment, carbon, and UV stages. Every drop that touches our plants comes from the sky. No chlorine, no fluoride, no municipal dependency. It's one of the things I'm most proud of in the whole operation.

Companion planting is where the garden becomes beautiful. Basil grows alongside cannabis in every bed — repels pests, attracts pollinators, and some growers believe it enhances terpene production. We also plant marigolds, lavender, and rosemary at the borders. The garden looks like a garden, smells incredible, and functions as a natural pest management system. No chemicals. Just plants helping plants.

---

The worm tea ritual is my favourite part. Every two weeks during the growing season, I brew aerobic compost tea — castings in a mesh bag, suspended in a bucket of water with an aquarium pump bubbling away. After 48 hours it's teeming with beneficial microorganisms. Applied as a foliar spray and soil drench, you can literally see the growth boost within days. It's deeply satisfying in a way that's hard to explain to anyone who hasn't watched a plant respond to something they made from worms and patience.

— THE HARVEST —

Flushing comes first — ten days before harvest, plain rainwater only. This forces the plant to consume its own stored nutrients, which means a cleaner, smoother smoke. The leaves start yellowing as the chlorophyll breaks down. Some growers panic when they see that. I smile. It means flushing is working.

Drying: the whole plant hangs upside down in a dark, ventilated room at 16–18°C, 55–60% humidity. Slow drying over 10–14 days preserves terpenes and prevents mould. I check daily. The test is simple: when the stems snap instead of bending, it's ready. Patience is everything at this stage.

---

Dry trimming is slower and more labour-intensive than wet trimming, but the quality difference is real. Trimming after drying preserves the trichomes — those tiny crystal structures that hold the cannabinoids and terpenes. It produces a tighter, more beautiful bud. It's the kind of detail most people wouldn't notice, but I notice. The meticulousness value isn't aspirational. It's just how my brain works.

Curing is where good flower becomes exceptional flower. Two months in airtight glass jars, burped daily for the first two weeks, then weekly. The moisture redistributes evenly, the chlorophyll breaks down completely, and the terpene profile develops its full complexity. I taste-test every week. The jars are labelled, dated, and stored in a cool, dark room. Opening a jar of properly cured flower after two months is one of the most satisfying moments in the entire growing calendar. The smell alone tells you whether you got it right.

— THE GRADES —

Quads are the top shelf — dense, trichome-covered, exceptional terpene profile. The best of the season. Opening a jar of quads is like opening a bottle of wine you've been saving. Trips are excellent — slightly less dense, but the flavour and effect are outstanding. This is what I smoke daily. Doubles are the solid everyday option. Good flower, grown with the same care. And then budget blend — smaller buds, less visual appeal, but perfect for edibles or pre-rolls. Nothing goes to waste. Every grade has its purpose.

— ASH'S GREEN FINGERS —

Six Cats is the outlet for what I call my green fingers — the deeply satisfying practice of growing something from seed to harvest. These are the same hands that paint neon faces at festivals. Both practices require the same qualities: patience, attention to detail, sensitivity to the subject, and a deep respect for the process. The cannabis plant and the human face both reward the artist who listens before they paint.

Six Cats was started with Barbara, who is also my partner at LightSpeed and one of my best friends. We've built two creative businesses together, which is either a testament to our partnership or our mutual stubbornness — probably both. Every season since 2019 has been a new chapter. The cats have come and gone. Lucy, our cross-Burmese matriarch, left us in October 2023. The green garden carries on.

What started as one man's garden has become a club, a community, and a philosophy. The eight values aren't just about cannabis — they're about how I approach everything. Authenticity in the art. Consciousness in the business. Consistency on the bicycle. Meticulousness in the code. Sustainability in the lifestyle. Passion in everything. Quality above all. Six Cats is not a side project. It's a mirror.
