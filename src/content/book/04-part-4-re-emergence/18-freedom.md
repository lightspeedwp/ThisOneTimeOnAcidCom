Freedom shows up everywhere in my life. Berlin: a city that demands difference. LightSpeed: remote-first, flexible hours, creative autonomy for the team. The bicycle: freedom of movement, no schedules, no dependency on infrastructure. Festival life: the dancefloor as the last truly free space. The art: no pre-planning, no Pinterest references, no client briefs. Pure spontaneous creation.

Freedom is something that many employees don't experience. The freedom given to the LightSpeed team helps them lead happier lives and ultimately benefits the company because they deliver better work. It's taken a long time and many years of change, adaptation, and adjustment to get to this point.

The team is levelling up week on week at a rate never seen before in my career. Yes, this is because of AI, but without a team that has the drive to learn, progress, and grow, this would not happen. I'm currently mentoring the entire team into a new age of AI-powered workflow: GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude, Model Context Protocol. The way AI has changed how we build things is something I never could have predicted even two years ago.

---

When we advertised for new developers, we posted on LinkedIn and received 450 applicants, of which only a handful properly read the requirements. We expressly did NOT want Elementor or page builder developers. We wanted people with a will to learn. Out of 450 applicants, three interns joined — Hugo Gravito, Brandon Marshall, and Seren van der Merwe. They are levelling up faster than anyone I've ever seen.

The process obsession at LightSpeed isn't despite ADHD; it's because of it. ADHD brains need structure to function, so I build meticulous systems — GitHub workflows, daily planning templates, curriculum structures — that keep the chaos productive. Radical delegation: giving the team freedom isn't just philosophy; it's necessity. An ADHD founder can't micromanage.

Right now LightSpeed has the most optimal workflow and team it has ever had in the twenty-three years the company has been going.
