## Your Body Is Your Primary Tool

I'm not a professional athlete. I'm an artist who happens to take endurance sports seriously.

Swim. Bike. Run. Repeat. Ten to fifteen hours per week dedicated to training my body like it's a high-performance machine. Because it is.

This video explores why triathlon training matters for creative practice, and how discipline and freedom are not opposites — they're symbiotic.

### The Three Disciplines

**Swimming:** The meditation discipline. In the water, there's no music, no distractions, just breath and rhythm. Bilateral breathing (every third stroke) creates meditative symmetry. The pool is where I process ideas.

**Cycling:** The endurance discipline. Long rides (80-120km) teach patience, pacing, and mental resilience. The body learns to manage discomfort. The mind learns to stay present through fatigue.

**Running:** The pain discipline. Running is simple but not easy. Every stride is a negotiation between willpower and physical limits. This is where mental toughness is built.

### Why Endurance Sports?

The answer isn't "to stay fit." That's a side benefit.

The real reason: **creative practice demands a body that can perform.**

Festival painting sessions last 12 hours. I'm standing the entire time, hunched over faces, arms extended, fine motor control under fatigue. If my body isn't conditioned, the art suffers.

Cycling to festivals with 40kg of gear requires leg strength, core stability, and cardiovascular endurance. If I'm not trained, the journey becomes suffering instead of meditation.

Muay Thai training in Thailand requires explosive power, flexibility, and recovery capacity. If my body isn't maintained, I can't train.

**The body is the tool. The tool needs maintenance.**

### Discipline Creates Freedom

This is the insight most people miss: discipline and freedom are not opposites.

Without the discipline of daily training, I don't have the freedom to:
- Paint for 12 hours without physical breakdown
- Cycle 300km to a festival on a whim
- Spend two hours in Muay Thai training every morning
- Dance until sunrise without injury

The more disciplined my training, the more freedom I have in my creative practice.

### The Training Schedule

Here's what a typical week looks like when I'm in full training mode:

**Monday:** Swim (technique work, 2km)  
**Tuesday:** Run (intervals, 10km)  
**Wednesday:** Bike (long ride, 80-100km)  
**Thursday:** Swim (endurance, 3km)  
**Friday:** Run (easy pace, 8km)  
**Saturday:** Bike + Run (brick workout, 60km + 5km)  
**Sunday:** Long run (15-20km)

Total: 10-15 hours per week. Every week. Year-round.

### The Mental Benefits

Endurance sports don't just build physical capacity. They build mental resilience.

**Lessons from triathlon training:**
- Discomfort is temporary. Keep moving.
- Pacing matters more than speed. Sustainable effort wins.
- The body is more capable than the mind believes.
- Rest is part of training, not a break from it.
- Consistency beats intensity.

These lessons apply directly to creative practice. The 12-hour painting session is an endurance event. The multi-day festival is an ultra-marathon. The discipline required is the same.

### The Body-Mind Connection

The ADHD brain needs movement to regulate. Sitting still is torture. Long training sessions provide the movement my nervous system craves.

After a two-hour bike ride, my mind is clear. Ideas that were stuck suddenly flow. Problems that seemed unsolvable resolve themselves.

Movement is thinking. Training is meditation.

### Not About Performance

I'm not trying to win races. I'm not chasing podiums. I'm training to maintain the machine that allows me to do the work I love.

Triathlon gives me:
- Physical capacity for long creative sessions
- Mental resilience for challenging projects
- Routine and structure for the ADHD brain
- A healthy outlet for competitive energy
- A community of disciplined, goal-oriented people

> Triathlon and Muay Thai: your body is your primary tool, and the tool needs maintenance. Discipline and freedom are not opposites.

This is why an artist trains like an athlete. Because the art demands it.
